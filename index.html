Case Study 2026

BRD – Barrel & Brew Craft Winery

A Business Requirements Document for a family-owned craft winery whose products had grown to over 500 local restaurants, but whose operations had not scaled with demand. The BRD defines requirements for a Brewery Management System to replace handwritten notebooks, an outdated shared spreadsheet and verbal production requests.

Problem
Missed weekly production targets and stockouts, inconsistent batch quality (two batches discarded), 30% higher overtime, an unreliable bottling line and sales promising stock without checking with production.
Analysis
Gap analysis across 7 operational areas, a fishbone diagram for issue categorisation, and as-is and to-be swimlane process maps.
Requirements
10 functional requirements (7 Must Have, 3 Should Have) and 6 non-functional requirements, prioritised with MoSCoW, covering recipe management, batch tracking, QC monitoring, inventory alerts, distributor sales integration, demand forecasting and real-time availability checks.
Also covers
Project objectives, scope, stakeholders, assumptions and constraints, risk register and success criteria (for example, overtime reduced by at least 50% and forecast accuracy within 10%).
Status
Proposed requirements only. The success criteria are targets, not achieved results.

Case Study 2024

Global HCM Business Case – Synergy Enterprise

A business case for consolidating the HR technology of a global FMCG manufacturer (75,000+ employees, five continents) from 20+ disconnected systems into one cloud HCM platform. It compares Workday HCM, SAP SuccessFactors and Oracle Fusion Cloud HCM against defined requirements and recommends Workday.

Problem
Fragmented employee data, manual HR processes, no global reporting, inconsistent employee experience, compliance exposure across 50+ countries and more than US$9M a year in system maintenance.
Analysis
Predetermined requirements, side-by-side vendor comparison, benefits beyond efficiency, 5-year cost-benefit analysis and a risk register with mitigations.
Financial projections
Estimated investment of US$52–71M over 5 years against US$30–50M a year in quantified benefits, giving a projected ROI of 250–350% and an 18–24 month payback.
Delivery approach
Phased rollout using the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) with 3-month Program Increments, a pilot region, regional waves and go/no-go gates. The business case argues against pure Waterfall and pure Scrum.
Status
Recommendation proposed only, requesting US$22–30M for the initial phase. Savings and ROI figures are benchmark-based projections, not realised results.

Case Study 2024

SmilePoint Dental Clinic – Online Appointment Booking Portal

A business analysis of a dental clinic that manages appointments by phone, paper books and spreadsheets, and a proposal for an online booking portal. The work identifies the business problems on both the patient and operational side and maps each stakeholder group's goals and concerns.

Problem
Long phone waits, no booking outside clinic hours, missed appointments, double-bookings, lost or illegible paper records and heavy reception workload.
Stakeholders analysed
Patients, reception and admin staff, dentists, clinic management, IT or vendor team and regulatory bodies, with goals and concerns for each (for example data privacy for patients and job-displacement fears for reception).
Proposed outcomes
24/7 self-service booking with real-time availability, automated SMS and email confirmations and reminders, and fewer manual errors.
Status
Proposed solution. Not presented as implemented, and no measured outcomes are claimed.

Analysis Exercise 2025

Lloyds Banking Group – Strategic Performance Analysis

A KPI dashboard analysing quarterly performance from 2020 to 2022 across three measures: Cost-to-Income Ratio, Customer Retention Rate and Return on Equity. Each metric is shown in its own chart so trends and quarter-to-quarter swings can be compared.

Cost-to-Income Ratio
Line chart, 2020 Q1 to 2022 Q3. The figure peaked at 6,186.2 in 2021 Q4, then dropped to 5,396.9 in 2022 Q1, its lowest point.
Customer Retention
Column chart, 2020 Q1 to 2022 Q4. Highest in 2020 Q2 (29,765 customers), lowest in 2022 Q1 (25,277).
Return on Equity
Area chart, 2020 Q1 to 2022 Q3. Values stayed in a narrow band of about 1.76M to 1.89M, with the high in 2020 Q4.
Context
Independent portfolio analysis; not an engagement commissioned by Lloyds Banking Group.
